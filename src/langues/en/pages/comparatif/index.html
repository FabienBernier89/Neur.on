Corrext and DeepL. the same engine, in a framework that protects professional secrecy

DeepL Pro is an excellent generic translation engine: fast, smooth, comfortable with Office documents and PDFs. It is still available in Corrext. What is changing is the framework around him.

  • What DeepL Pro does well: fluidity, formats, its own glossaries, a public commitment to deleting the texts of its Pro customers.
  • What he does not provide alone: treatment in Switzerland, a deaf Swiss-law composition officer, a chain of legal review.
  • Our position: Don't give up DeepL Pro, use it under your organization's governance.

Corrext and generalist LLMs. remarkable to understand, insufficient alone to commit

ChatGPT, Azure OpenAI GPT and Claude reformulate, summarize and explain a legal transition with ease that no conventional translation engine can achieve. The problem is not there: it is in what they cannot prove.

  • What they do well: reformulate, summarize, explain, adapt to the registry, work with the ambiguity of a clause.
  • What they do not provide alone: sourced Swiss terminology, verifiable legal reference, quotation, commissioned reinterpretation.
  • One fact we document: no engine is a source of law, including ours.

Our method of comparison

These pages are written by Neur.on, the Corrext editor. We therefore do not claim to be neutral: we have to be verifiable. Each row in each table shows a criterion that you can control yourself, in both tools, without believing us by word.

What we compare

The place of storage and the place where the data is processed, the existence of a warning before a document is sent, the terminology of Swiss law that can be viewed in context, the management of Swiss legal references, the possibility of comparing several engines, the human review ordered in the tool, the quote and the deadline, certified translation, formats and deployment.

What we are not comparing

Prices: they depend on your volume, languages and level of replay, and a comparison grid would not make any sense out of context. Gross velocity: all of the engines cited reflect in a matter of seconds, the distance does not decide anything. The quality “in general” is not measured by a sentence.

What we claim from others

Only what is public and stable at the verification date: the generic nature of an engine, the location of its infrastructure, the absence of an integrated legal review chain, the absence of a sourced Swiss law concordant. When a point appeared to us to be uncertain, we nuanced it rather than decided it.

What we claim to Corrext

Only what has been observed in the App. Functions not seen in the interface are not promised on this site. product truth found in Corrext.

Frequently asked questions about these comparatives

Are these comparisons neutral?

No, and we prefer to say this: they are written by Neur.on, the Corrext publisher. That's precisely why they stick to criteria that you can check yourself: place of storage and data processing, terminology of Swiss law in context, management of legal references, presence of a review chain, quotation and certification. We do not compare prices or gross speed.

can Corrext use DeepL Pro, Azure OpenAI GPT or Claude?

Yes. These engines are available in Corrext, alongside LexMachina, and are selectable for each shipment. As they are hosted abroad, their selection triggers the Attorney-Client privilege opinion of your organization, which requires full pseudonymization of the document. Our proposal is therefore not to give up these engines, but to use them in a framework that protects your professional secrecy.

What is the basis for these comparisons?

Everything we claim to Corrext has been observed in the app. What we say about other solutions is limited to what is public and stable. Examples of translation are real, one-off exits, never statistical measurements.
