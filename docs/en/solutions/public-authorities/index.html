Authorities and public administration. Nothing leaves Switzerland

A ruling to notify in the party's language. A report to publish in three official languages. A file to send to a foreign authority. Corrext works with the terminology of published texts and keeps processing in Switzerland.

What Corrext changes for a public administration

A Swiss administration does not translate to inform. It translates because the law provides for it, into languages that all have the same standing. So the question is not how fluent the result reads, but how faithful it is to the official text and who controls the data flow.

Three uses in the public sector

Courts. Notifying in the party's language

The text is translated with procedural terminology, checked term by term against official sources, then reviewed by a legal translator before notification.

Administrations. Publishing in three official languages

A report becomes a project: three target languages, one review level and a delivery time shown before you confirm.

Mutual assistance. Sending a file abroad

The documents go into a project with the review and certification the receiving authority requires. Commercial register extracts can be ordered online, certified, notarised or with an apostille.

Can a public administration use an online translation service?

An authority's data is often subject to cantonal or federal legislation on data protection and official secrecy. Corrext makes the data flow explicit: you choose the confidentiality mode and the engine, and the application shows the consequences of that choice before sending.

Frequently asked questions from the public sector

Does the data stay in Switzerland?

Storage is Swiss in every case. Processing is too, in Highly sensitive content mode or with the LexMachina engine. Third-party engines, hosted abroad, trigger an explicit warning before anything is sent.

Is the terminology of official texts respected?

The CHnell concordancer aligns segments from Fedlex, the Federal Gazette and federal administration publications. Each occurrence shows its source and opens in context.

Is deployment on our own servers possible?

Yes, on-premises deployment is available for the most sensitive environments. The scope is defined in a technical discussion.

How do I obtain a certified translation for a foreign authority?

For a commercial register extract, the online service offers three levels: certification by Corrext, notarised certification, and notarised certification followed by an apostille under the Hague Convention of 5 October 1961.

Which languages are covered?

The platform covers 30 languages. German, French, Italian and English are native and are the four languages available in Highly sensitive content mode.

Are documents used to train models?

No. Client content is not used to train Neur.on's translation models, and projects in Highly sensitive content mode are not reused as translation memory.
