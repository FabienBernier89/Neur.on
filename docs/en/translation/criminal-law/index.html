Translating criminal proceedings, in practice

Translating in Swiss criminal law means carrying over procedural documents whose legal nature is precise: summary penalty order, indictment, ruling abandoning proceedings, decision on detention. The Swiss Criminal Code (SCC) and the Criminal Procedure Code (CrimPC) are published in German, French and Italian. Each document has an official name in each of these languages.

The acts criminal proceedings cite every day

Swiss criminal law rests on a substantive code and a procedural code unified since 2011. International mutual assistance and the fight against money laundering add their own acts and their own vocabulary.

SCC
Swiss Criminal Code

It defines offences and penalties, from offences against property to money laundering; StGB in German, CP in French and Italian.

CrimPC
Swiss Criminal Procedure Code

It governs the investigation, procedural acts, detention and trial in every canton; StPO in German, CPP in French and Italian.

IMAC
Federal Act on International Mutual Assistance in Criminal Matters

It governs requests made to Switzerland, or by Switzerland to a foreign state; IRSG in German, EIMP in French.

AMLA
Federal Act on Combating Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing

It sets the obligations whose breach gives rise to many white-collar crime proceedings; GwG in German, LBA in French, LRD in Italian.

Three stages of criminal proceedings that require translation

Defence. An investigation in another language

The case is investigated in German and the accused is defended by a lawyer from French-speaking Switzerland. Batches go into File translation in Highly sensitive content mode, and the defence reads everything before choosing which documents to have reviewed.

Time limit. Ten days to contest a summary penalty order

The time limit for a rejection leaves no time to wait for an external translation. The text is pasted straight into Text and document translation, the decision is taken the same day, and only the rejection actually filed goes through a legal translator.

International. A request for mutual assistance

The request must be understood by a foreign authority while staying true to the legal classifications of Swiss law. The concordancer shows the terms of the SCC and the CrimPC in context, and the version sent goes through a Full review.

Frequently asked questions on criminal law translation

Can a machine translation be filed in the case record?

Its first purpose is to help you understand the case. A document filed with the criminal justice authority must be reliable and, depending on the case, attested. It then goes through a Full review by a legal translator, or Double review & certified translation when the court requires a certified translation.

How do I protect the confidentiality of the investigation?

Storage is on Swiss servers in every case. Highly sensitive content mode also requires processing in Switzerland only, with no reuse of the content in a translation memory. Engines hosted abroad display a warning before anything is sent.

Are the names of procedural documents harmonised?

The Criminal Procedure Code has applied throughout Switzerland since 2011 and is published in the three official languages, which fixes the name of each document. The concordancer shows these occurrences in context, with their source, in the Criminal Law and Litigation & Enforcement domains.

Can I process a case file of several thousand pages?

Yes, in batches: up to 25 files of 50 MB per upload in Text and document translation, or through a translation project for end-to-end tracking with quote and review. Documents come back in their original format, which preserves exhibit numbering.

What about scanned or poor-quality documents?

The PDF to Word tab converts a scanned PDF into an editable Word document before translation or annotation. This is common for seized documents, old correspondence and annexes sent as images by a foreign authority. The resulting document is then translated like a native file.

Which languages for international proceedings?

German, French, Italian and English are native in every pair, and the platform covers 30 languages in total. The legal classifications, however, remain those of Swiss law, which the concordancer lets you check article by article. Documents come back in their original format, whatever the source language.
