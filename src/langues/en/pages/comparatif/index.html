Corrext and DeepL. The same engine, within a framework that protects professional secrecy

DeepL Pro is an excellent general-purpose translation engine: fast, fluent and at ease with Office documents and PDFs. It remains available in Corrext. What changes is the framework around it.

  • What DeepL Pro does well: fluency, formats, its own glossaries, and a public commitment to delete its Pro customers' texts.
  • What it does not provide on its own: processing in Switzerland, a Swiss law concordancer with sources, a legal review chain.
  • Our position: do not give up DeepL Pro, use it under your organisation's governance.

Corrext and general-purpose LLMs. Remarkable for understanding, not enough on their own for binding documents

ChatGPT, Azure OpenAI GPT and Claude rephrase, summarise and explain a legal passage with an ease no conventional translation engine can match. That is not where the problem lies. It lies in what they cannot prove.

  • What they do well: rephrasing, summarising, explaining, adapting to the register, working through the ambiguity of a clause.
  • What they do not provide on their own: sourced Swiss terminology, a verifiable legal reference, a quote, an ordered review.
  • A fact we document: no engine is a source of law, ours included.

Translation agency or platform. Two ways of working, not two camps

A legal translation agency provides what no software replaces: contractual liability, a named translator, certification. Corrext does not try to replace it. Its project workflow is precisely where you order that external review.

  • What an agency brings: liability, a human relationship, certification, expertise in binding documents.
  • What a generic tool brings: immediacy, to read and understand.
  • What Corrext combines: immediacy first, human review only for the documents that require it.

Where data is stored and where it is processed, whether a warning appears before a document is sent, Swiss legal terminology available in context, the handling of Swiss legal references, the ability to compare several engines, human review ordered in the tool, the quote and delivery time, certified translation, formats and deployment.

What we do not compare

Prices: they depend on your volume, your languages and your review level, and a side-by-side price list would mean nothing out of context. Raw speed: all the engines cited translate in a few seconds, and the difference decides nothing. Quality ‘in general’: it cannot be measured on a single sentence.

Frequently asked questions about these comparisons

Are these comparisons neutral?

No, and we would rather say so: they are written by Neur.on, the publisher of Corrext. That is precisely why they stick to criteria you can check yourself: where data is stored and processed, Swiss legal terminology in context, the handling of legal references, and whether there is a review chain, a quote and certification. We compare neither prices nor raw speed.

Can Corrext use DeepL Pro, Azure OpenAI GPT or Claude?

Yes. These engines are available in Corrext alongside LexMachina, and you choose one each time you send a text. Because they are hosted abroad, selecting one triggers your organisation's Attorney-Client privilege notice, which requires the document to be fully pseudonymised. So we are not asking you to give up these engines. We suggest using them within a framework that protects your professional secrecy.

What are these comparisons based on?

Everything we say about Corrext has been observed in the application. What we say about other solutions is limited to what is public and stable. The translation examples are real, one-off outputs, never statistical measurements.
