in brief

In Switzerland, the terminology of a contract is not a matter of style. The laws are published in German, French and Italian, and all three versions are authoritative. The translator therefore does not seek an equivalent: he reproduces the one already adopted by the legislator.

A contract drafted in French and executed in Zurich, a German shareholders' agreement governed by Swiss law, an English distribution agreement referring to the Swiss Code of obligations: in each of these cases, the translation does not consist in finding a correct word. It is to find the word that the Confederation has already written in the right language and not to change it.

Why three versions are also authoritative

German, French and Italian are the official languages of the Confederation; Romansh is the fourth national language, and the Confederation publishes texts of particular importance in Romansh. In concrete terms, federal legislation is published simultaneously in German, French and Italian, and all three versions have the same legal force. None is presented as a translation of another.

The practical consequence is simple, and it often surprises lawyers coming from a monolingual system: when you translate a contract that quotes CO Art. 104, you do not translate “default interest”. You will search for the word used in Article 104 in the German (Verzugszinse) or Italian (Interessi moratori) version of the same article. The term already exists. Proposing otherwise, even with a perfectly correct synonym, introduces a discrepancy with the text that the court will read.

The second consequence is less well-known: in case of a discrepancy between the three versions, the interpretation does not consist in preferring the “original” version, since there is no such version. The reasoning confronts the three texts in order to establish the desired meaning. A bilingual contract that faithfully incorporates the official terminology of the two languages concerned is therefore on the right side of this exercise.

What to remember for a contract. Whenever your text refers to a legal provision, translation of the reference is not free. It is determined by the corresponding official version. The rest of the contract remains editorial work.

Abbreviations are not translated: they change

This is the most common and visible error for a Swiss reader. Abbreviations of laws are not international abbreviations: each official language has its own and is not inferred from the translated name. A generalist translation engine which renders “CO” by “CO” in German produces a legible phrase and a false reference.

There are three traps behind this picture.

  • the form of the subdivision also changes. French is written “art. 104 para. 1 CO”, German is written “art. 104 ABS. 1 OR”, Italian is written “art. 104 CPV. 1 CO”. A sub-paragraph becomes Absatz and then CAPOVERSO; a letter becomes Buchstabe and then lettering. A reference translated word-to-word leaves “al.” in German and is immediately visible.
  • identical abbreviations are reassuring false friends. CO and CC are the same in French and Italian: you get used to not touching it, and you forget to change it to German.
  • competing abbreviations exist. the Banking Act is cited in LB in French and LBCR in Italian; both circulate in practice. Faced with such a discrepancy, the only way out is to open the official version and read the abbreviation in the text itself.

English: essential in practice, without legal value

English is not an official language of the Confederation. However, it is the working language of many transactions, international arbitration and financial documentation. This tension gives rise to two misunderstandings.

The first concerns English translations of federal laws available on the official portal: they are provided for information purposes and are not authentic. They are useful to understand, they cannot justify an argument. A contract that quotes “Article 104(1) of the Swiss Code of obligations” actually cites convenience of reading, not an official text.

The second relates to the very form of the quotation in English. Several agreements co-exist: “Art. 104 para. 1 CO”, “Art. 104(1) of the Swiss Code of obligations”, “CO Art. 104 para. 1”. None of them is at fault. What is it is to mix them in one document, or let a tool produce a different one in each paragraph. Choose a Convention, write it in your style guide and have it applied by everyone.

Security rule. in an English-speaking contract governed by Swiss law, keep the official abbreviation of the reference language (CO, GOLD or CO) and add, once, the English language developed in brackets. The English-speaking reader understands, and the reference remains verifiable.

Traditional traps, language by language

German from Switzerland

  • the ss does not exist. gemäss, Grösse, Strasse, never gemäss are written. A document containing a single ff. indicates that it has been produced or reproduced with a German-regulated correction.
  • the thousands are separated by an apostrophe: 15,000, 1,250,000. The point or fine space betrays a foreign origin.
  • the vocabulary of the companies differs. AG and GmbH are common Swiss forms, but the governing bodies, registers and proceedings bear their own names under Swiss law; the nearest German equivalent is often another institution.
  • the highest court is Bundesgericht, never Bundesgerichtshof, which is a German court. Confusion is common in automatic exits and is immediately disqualifying.

Italian of Switzerland

  • the company limited by shares is said to be Société AnonIMA in Switzerland, where Italy writes Società per azioni. Likewise, Sàrl becomes Sagl and not S.r.l.
  • homonymous institutions do not designate the same thing. Consiglio di Stato is the cantonal government in Ticino, whereas in Italy it is an administrative jurisdiction. Translating each other changes the meaning of an entire sentence.
  • the Supreme Court is Federal Supreme Court and the published judgments are Federal Supreme Court. Corte di cassazione belongs to another legal order.
  • abbreviations of laws follow the Swiss nomenclature (LEF, LBCR, LDIP), not Italian codes.

Formats, amounts and dates

  • The currency is written as CHF, usually before the amount, with the apostrophe as a separator of the thousands.
  • The date is usually written 24.09.2026 in all three languages; in English, the same date becomes ambiguous if you leave it in numbers. Write down the month in words.
  • Percentages, rates and deadlines are data to be verified number by number after any automatic translation: these are the most expensive and barely visible errors in fast replay.

Check a term in an official source within three minutes

The method takes four steps and does not require a subscription.

  1. Open the deed on Fedlex, the Federal Law Portal. Each act is available in German, French and Italian. Switch from one language to another to the same article: you read the official term in its context, not a dictionary definition.
  2. write down the abbreviation as it appears at the top of the text. It is the only source that separates between two competing abbreviations.
  3. seek confirmed use in published judgments of the Federal Supreme Court, in the Federal Gazette or, in the case of regulated areas, in publications of the authority concerned. A term used by lawmakers and adopted by the courts is a secure term.
  4. for an isolated term without a connection clause, the Confederation's terminology database provides the equivalents used by the Federal Administration in the four national languages and in English.

What costs time is not research: it is redoing it. Keep a record of each terminology decision along with the source in a file shared by the team. After three files, you have a home glossary that is better than any generic list because it is linked to your texts and customers.

Checklist before signing a translation

  • Each legal removal has been reopened in the official version of the target language.
  • Abbreviations of laws correspond to the target language, including footnotes and appendices.
  • Subdivisions (subsection, letter, subsection) use the nomenclature of the target language.
  • The names of jurisdictions, authorities and registers are Swiss, not their foreign homonymous names.
  • No syllables in German; thousands separator by apostrophe.
  • The amounts, rates, dates and deadlines have been re-computed on the original.
  • The same source term is given by the same target term throughout the document, including annexes.
  • Contract definitions follow the same rules as the body of the text.

Frequently asked Questions

Which version of a Swiss federal act is authoritative?

Federal legislation shall be published in German, French and Italian, and all three versions shall be equally valid. None is the official translation of another: in case of a discrepancy, the interpretation is made by confronting the three. Therefore, the terminology of a contract governed by Swiss law must reproduce the terminology of the official version of the chosen language, and not an equivalence found elsewhere.

How does the German and Italian Code of obligations abbreviate?

CO in French, GOLD (Obligationenrecht) in German, CO (Codice delle obbligazioni) in Italian. The Civil Code becomes ZGB in German and CC in Italian. The debt enforcement and bankruptcy Act is abbreviated to LP in French, SchKG in German and LEF in Italian. An abbreviation does not translate: it replaces the abbreviation of the corresponding official version.

Are English translations of Swiss laws legally valid?

No. English is not an official language of the Confederation. English versions of federal texts published on the Fedlex portal are for information purposes and are not authoritative. In an English-language contract governed by Swiss law, quote the article in the official language of reference and indicate, if you wish, a courtesy translation in brackets.

Which formal traps are most often found in German and Italian in Switzerland?

In Swiss German, the ss does not exist: one always writes ff. the thousands divide by an apostrophe. In Italian of Switzerland, several institutions have a different name from their Italian homonymous: the company limited by shares is said to be a company limited by shares and not a company limited by shares per azioni, and the Ticino Council of State is not the Italian Consiglio di Stato. The name of the Supreme Court is the Federal Supreme Court, Bundesgericht, Federal Supreme Court, never the name of a foreign court.

Where can a Swiss legal term be checked free of charge in all three languages?

On Fedlex, the Federal Law Portal, which displays the same document article by article in German, French and Italian. For a single term, the Confederation's terminology database, TERMDAT, and the published judgments of the Federal Supreme Court have proven their use. The specialist areas are supplemented by the Federal Gazette, FINMA publications and cantonal portals.
