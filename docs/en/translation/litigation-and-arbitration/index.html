Translating proceedings, in practice

Translating for litigation and arbitration means producing two kinds of text: exhibits to understand in order to build a strategy, and written submissions or awards meant to be read, cited and enforced. The vocabulary comes from the Civil Procedure Code (CPC), the Federal Act on Private International Law (PILA) and the case law of the Federal Supreme Court.

Procedural vocabulary in four languages

Appellate remedies change name with the court. A cantonal appeal (Berufung, appel) and an appeal to the Federal Supreme Court (Beschwerde, recours) have different names, and the three official versions keep the distinction. The English column uses the translation published by the Confederation on Fedlex where there is one, which has no legal force, and common usage otherwise.

The acts proceedings cite every day

Since 2011, Swiss civil procedure has been codified in a single code that applies in every canton. International arbitration has a chapter of its own, and the enforcement of monetary claims falls under yet another act.

CPC
Swiss Civil Procedure Code

It governs written submissions, evidence, time limits and the cantonal appellate remedies; ZPO in German, CPC in French and Italian.

PILA
Federal Act on Private International Law

Its Chapter 12, International Arbitration, is the core of international arbitration law in Switzerland; IPRG in German, LDIP in French and Italian.

BGG
Federal Supreme Court Act

It sets the requirements for an appeal to the highest Swiss court and the form of written submissions. Fedlex has no English translation: the English version of the DEBA uses this title. BGG in German, LTF in French.

DEBA
Federal Act on Debt Enforcement and Bankruptcy

It governs the enforcement of monetary claims, from the setting aside of the objection (Rechtsöffnung) to bankruptcy; SchKG in German, LP in French, LEF in Italian.

Three stages of proceedings where translation matters

Preparation. Four hundred pages of exhibits before the hearing

The batch goes into File translation in Highly sensitive content mode and comes back in its original format. The team reads, annotates and flags the useful passages, then sends for human review only the exhibits that will be filed.

Written submissions. Prayers for relief in the language of the court

The written submission is drafted in English and must be filed in German. A Full review by a legal translator checks the prayers for relief line by line, and Double review & certified translation covers cases where the court requires a certificate.

Arbitration. An award to be recognised in Switzerland

Recognition turns on a faithful operative part. The alternatives from each engine show the possible wordings, CHnell gives the official usage of the term in the PILA, and the partner makes an informed choice.

Frequently asked questions on translation for proceedings

Will a Swiss court accept a machine translation?

An exhibit filed with a court must be reliable and, depending on the case, attested. Machine translation helps you understand the case file. What you file goes through a Full review by a legal translator, or Double review & certified translation when an attestation is required.

Which language do exhibits have to be translated into?

Into the official language of the court, which depends on the canton and on the authority hearing the case. German, French and Italian are native in every pair, and so is English. That also covers international arbitrations conducted in English with exhibits in German.

How do I keep the case file secret?

Storage is on Swiss servers in every case. Highly sensitive content mode also requires processing in Switzerland only, with no reuse of the content in a translation memory. Engines hosted abroad display a warning before anything is sent.

Can I compare several translations of the same passage?

Yes. The Alternatives panel offers up to four wordings, each labelled with the engine that produced it. This helps when quoting an award or case law, where two acceptable wordings may not carry the same weight before the next court. The wording you choose can then be fixed in your terminology resources for the whole case file.

How can I check a procedural term?

Highlight it: Fast lookup CHnell opens it in context with its official source, in the Litigation & Enforcement and Arbitration domains. The concordancer accepts quotation marks, the minus sign and the ‘or’ operator to target a precise occurrence.

What about poor-quality scanned exhibits?

The PDF to Word tab in Text and document translation converts a scan into an editable Word document before translation or annotation. This is often the first step for a list of exhibits, where some annexes exist only as images. The resulting Word document is then translated like any native file.
