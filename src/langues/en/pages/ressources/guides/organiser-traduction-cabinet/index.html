1. The current situation: counting before deciding

Any attempt to organize the translation that starts with choosing a tool fails. It fails because it answers a question no one asked, and because it does not know the actual volume. Start with a four-week statement, without judgment and without sanction.

Ask each person to note, for each translation made or ordered: the date, language pair, type of document, end recipient, tool or service provider used, and an estimate of the time spent. A shared table is sufficient; the objective is not the accounting accuracy, it is the disclosure of an order of magnitude.

There are almost always three observations.

  • the internal part is the majority. the volume entrusted to a service provider, the volume known to the firm, is the visible part. The essentials are produced internally, as a matter of urgency, and are not invoiced anywhere.
  • the tools are heterogeneous. three or four different services coexist, chosen by individual custom rather than by decision.
  • No one knows what has already been translated. the same clauses, definitions, terms and conditions are translated from case to case.

How to present the statement. never present it as a compliance audit. Present it as a measure of the amount of time the firm devotes to work that it does not bill. The discussion changes in nature and the statement completes.

2. Mapping needs: Understanding, producing, filing

The quality of a translation is not discussed in the abstract. It is discussed in relation to a recipient. So, classify your needs into three families, and one question is sufficient to fit any document: who will read it?

FamilyRecipientDominant requirementWhat does n't matter
Understanding You, your team Speed and accuracy of sense; no omission Style, formatting, idiomatism
produce Customer, opponent, Partner Terminology consistency, tone, document keeping Extreme speed, if it costs consistency
file Authority, Court, publication Binding accuracy, confirmation by a third party, formalities Unit cost in relation to risk

This grid immediately makes legible something that internal debates are blurring: the majority of the volume belongs to the first family, which needs neither stylistic revision nor certification. It is also the family that invests in unauthorized services due to lack of tools. The second family is the one where a practice judges itself. The third is rare, costly, and is planned.

Then refer to this grid the expected level of replay. The three widely offered external levels naturally align themselves with this: a light review to understand and disseminate internally, a complete review for what comes out of the practice, a certified double review for what is deposited. The details of these levels are explained in our article on the reading of a quotation.

3. Who decides what

The most frequent blockage is not technical; it is a decision-making process: anyone may request a translation, no one is responsible for the level chosen, and the arbitration is done over the telephone, file by file. Three roles are enough to get out of it.

The member or lawyer responsible for the case

He is the only one who knows the recipient and the risk. It therefore decides on the family of needs and the level of review, and validates the terminology specific to the file. In order for this decision to cost it nothing, the written rule must give it an answer in default: it only arbitrates the exceptions.

The Secretariat or the file assistant

It runs: file storage, choice of target language, time tracking, retrieval, and categorization of the Deliverable. It does not decide on the level of review and does not have to interpret a privacy policy. Give him a procedure, not a judgment.

The reference Translation

A person, not a Committee. It keeps the glossary of the firm, follows the indicators, arbitrates cross-cutting terminological disagreements and remains the service provider's point of contact. In a medium-sized practice, this is a burden of a few hours per month, and its absence is the main reason for abandoning a well-designed process.

a simple test. take the last five translations from the practice and ask, for each, who has decided on the level of replay. If the answer is “person” or “the person who called the service provider”, the division of roles does not yet exist.

4. The rules to write and the rules not to write

A useful translation policy can be found on a front page. Beyond that, it is not read, so it does not exist. This is what it should contain.

  1. the default family of need and its preview level, for the five or six most common types of documents in the practice.
  2. the confidentiality method to be used according to the sensitivity of the file, formulated as a choice and not as a prohibition: in such cases, the strictest form; in other words, fashion.
  3. the trusted tool, named, with the name of the person giving access.
  4. the terminology validation circuit: which decides, in how long, and where the decision is recorded.
  5. the minimum time required depending on the family of need, for translation to cease to be a matter of urgency.
  6. the touchpoint when a case does not fall into any box.

What should not be written is just as important. Do not write down a list of prohibited tools: it is expired the following month and turns the policy into a policy document. Do not write a rule that starts with “it is strictly forbidden to” without indicating immediately what to do instead. And don't write down a numerical threshold that nobody can check.

5. Stopping Wild translations

A wildlife translation is referred to as pasting a file extract into an unapproved online service. This is the most concrete risk of a translation firm, and it does not deal with sanctioning, because it arises from a legitimate need: to understand quickly.

Three levers work in this order.

  • an approved tool at least as fast as the alternative. If the cabinet tool requests a written request and two validations to translate a paragraph, it will not be used. The comparison is not with a provider, but with a free service on a tab.
  • the rule displayed at the time of selection. a policy recalled in the interface, when the user selects an engine, is read. the same policy in an intranet is not. We describe this mechanism in our article on the treatment location.
  • an explicit exit door. for cases where the tool is not enough, specify who to call. The lack of documented appeal is what leads to improvisation.

A fourth lever, often neglected: explain once, seriously, why the question arises. the difference between storage and processing, the real scope of pseudonymisation, the notion of subcontracting: thirty minutes of presentation to the entire firm make more than a year of reminders by e-mail.

6. Capitalizing on terminology

A practice is constantly retranslating the same things. The definitions of its model contracts, the titles of its usual clauses, the names of its institutional clients, the legal references of its preferred areas. Each translation is time lost and an opportunity for inconsistency.

Capitalization does not require a sophisticated start-up tool.

  1. Open a shared file with four columns: Source term, target term, official decision source, date. Nothing else.
  2. Please only report it when a decision has been taken, not preventatively. A glossary built in advance is never consulted; a glossary that records real arbitrations is used.
  3. Remove the source. an entry without a verifiable reference will be challenged at the first disagreement, and you will resume discussion.
  4. Read it once a quarter with the referrer to eliminate duplications and resolve contradictions.

For the purposes of Swiss law, official sources must be used to verify: the Federal Law Portal, which provides the same article in the three languages, the published rulings and the publications of the supervisory authorities. A bilingual arrangement supported by these sources, such as fast lookup CHnell, makes this control immediate rather than cumbersome. The underlying reasoning is detailed in our article on legal quadrilingualism.

7. Measure, in six indicators

A process that is not measured becomes a sum of individual arrangements in two quarters. Six indicators are sufficient, collected at the same time every quarter, on a single page.

  1. volume processed per family of needs (understand, produce, deposit), in standard pages. This is the only unit that remains stable when formats change.
  2. part processed in the approved tool. the best governance indicator: If it does not progress, the rule is not practicable.
  3. actual delay between request and delivery, distinct from the stated delay. The difference between the two leads to circumvention.
  4. recovered internal review hours. as long as this line is zero, any arbitrage between internal and external review is distorted.
  5. copies after delivery: number of documents returned for correction, and dominant pattern. Three reasons are sufficient to classify: terminology, omission, formatting.
  6. portion of the files processed in the strictest confidentiality method, in relation to the portion of files that required it according to the written rule.

Do not follow the stated satisfaction: it is stable and does not say anything. Do not follow the unit cost alone, because it reduces mechanically when quality falls. Indicators 3, 4 and 5 read together give a much more accurate picture of what translation actually costs the firm.

Realistic timetable

  • months 1: statement of living and needs mapping.
  • month 2: drafting the rules page, designating the referrer, choosing the approved tool, and opening access.
  • months 3: presentation to the office, opening of the glossary, first reading of indicators that will serve as a basis for comparison.
  • following quarters: statement, Glossary Review, Rule Page adjustment. No more than one hour per quarter.

Frequently asked Questions

Where do I start organizing translation in a practice?

By the inventory of fixtures, over four weeks and without judgment: who translates, what, with what tool, for which recipient. Most firms discover at this stage that the majority of translations are made internally, as a matter of urgency, without anyone counting them. As long as this volume remains invisible, there will be no rules.

How do I classify a practice's translation needs?

In three families defined by the recipient: understand (for yourself and for the team), produce (for a client or an opposing party), file (for an authority, court or publication). Each family calls for a different level of review, a different time period and a different confidentiality method. Classification by recipient avoids endless debates on quality in general.

How can wild translations be stopped?

By giving an approved tool faster than the unauthorised alternative, and by displaying the rule at the time of selection rather than in a Charter. A ban without a replacement moves the problem out of your sight: employees then use public services that do not warn anything and leave no traces.

What indicators should guide legal translation?

The volume processed per family of needs, the portion processed in the approved tool, the actual time between request and delivery, the number of internal review hours recovered, the recoveries after delivery and the portion of files processed in the strictest confidentiality method. Six indicators are sufficient, provided that they are collected at the same time every quarter.

Who should decide on the level of replay of a document?

The partner or legal agent responsible for the case, because he is the only one who knows the recipient and the risk. The written rule must give him an answer in default for each family of need, so that he only has to adjudicate on special cases. The Secretariat executes, does not decide on the level.
