Arbitration · Art. 186 PILA

SOURCE · FRENCHLe tribunal arbitral statue sur sa propre compétence conformément à l'art. 186 LDIP, et la sentence peut faire l'objet d'un recours au Tribunal fédéral.

LEXMACHINA · ENGLISH

The arbitral tribunal shall decide on its own jurisdiction in accordance with art. 186 PILA, and the award may be appealed to the Federal Tribunal.

DEEPL PRO · ENGLISH

The arbitral tribunal shall rule on its own jurisdiction in accordance with Article 186 of the LDIP, and the award may be appealed to the Federal Supreme Court.

Both sentences are readable. One cites the article in its official English form, PILA, the abbreviation the Confederation uses for the Federal Act on Private International Law. The other keeps the French abbreviation LDIP in an English text. In a written submission for a court or an English-speaking party, that is the line a partner corrects by hand. For the court, it is the other way round: DeepL Pro uses its official English name, Federal Supreme Court, where LexMachina writes Federal Tribunal. On the same sentence, Azure OpenAI GPT also gives PILA. No engine is right every time. That is exactly why Corrext compares them in front of you instead of imposing one.

Why a specialised engine rather than a general-purpose one

Not because it would be ‘better’ in absolute terms, but because Swiss law has a rare feature: it is already published in four languages, and that material can be learned.

The official terminology already exists

The Confederation publishes its laws in German, French and Italian, and a large share of its texts in English. An engine trained on these texts does not look for a plausible equivalent: it uses the term of the official text. ‘Verzugszins’ becomes ‘intérêt moratoire’ in French, the term of the federal acts, and ‘default interest’ in their English translation.

Legal references have a structure

‘Art. 104 al. 1 CO’ in French is written ‘Art. 104 Abs. 1 OR’ in German and ‘Art. 104 para. 1 CO’ in English. LDIP becomes IPRG, then PILA. An engine that has seen millions of these correspondences returns them in their official form instead of translating the abbreviation word for word.

Consistency holds across the whole document

In a 60-page contract, a concept must keep the same name from the first page to the last. That is the job of a vocabulary learned from consistent texts. It is also the job of the Alternatives panel, which lets you settle on a wording and then stick to it.

Frequently asked questions about LexMachina

What is LexMachina trained on?

On Swiss legal and financial data: over 15 million multilingual segments from Federal Supreme Court decisions (BGE/ATF), FINMA publications, and the federal codes and acts. Never on our clients' documents.

Which languages does LexMachina cover?

German, French, Italian and English, natively and in every language pair: the four languages in which lawyers in Switzerland write and argue. The Corrext platform offers 30 languages in total. Beyond these four, a third-party engine handles the translation.

Where does the engine run?

In Switzerland, on Neur.on's Swiss infrastructure. Choosing LexMachina is enough to keep processing in Switzerland, even without Highly sensitive content mode. No pseudonymisation notice appears, because nothing leaves the country.

Are my documents used to train the engine?

No. LexMachina learns from public Swiss legal and financial texts, not from your files. In Standard mode, your projects feed a translation memory that only your organisation can use. In Highly sensitive content mode, no translation memory is fed.

Can I use engines other than LexMachina?

Yes. Corrext is model-agnostic: DeepL Pro, Azure OpenAI GPT and Claude are available in the same interface, and you choose the engine for each request. Engines hosted abroad trigger your organisation's Attorney-Client privilege notice.

How do I compare engines on my own texts?

In Text and document translation, the spark button opens the Alternatives panel: up to four suggestions for the same sentence, each labelled with its engine. Generate more gives you further options. You judge on your own texts rather than on a promise.
