Corrext and DeepL. The same engine, within a framework that protects professional secrecy

DeepL Pro is a very good general-purpose translation engine, and it is available in Corrext. This page does not try to turn you away from it. It sets out what DeepL Pro does well, then exactly where the difference lies for a Swiss legal matter.

The short answer

Yes, DeepL Pro can translate a legal text: it is a fast, fluent engine. In Corrext, it remains available, but under your organisation's governance. The difference is not fluency. It lies in where data is processed, in Swiss legal terminology and in the review chain.

Questions this page answers: DeepL for legal translation · DeepL and professional secrecy for lawyers · a Swiss alternative to DeepL.

What DeepL Pro does well

It would be dishonest to claim otherwise: DeepL Pro is one of the best general-purpose translation engines on the market, and many legal experts use it every day to good effect.

  • FluencyIts output reads naturally, even in long sentences with subordinate clauses, which are the daily fare of a statute or a contract.
  • FormatsIt translates Office documents and PDFs while keeping most of the layout, which saves rebuilding a document by hand.
  • GlossariesIt lets you define your own term pairs, so that a specific word is translated the same way from one document to the next.
  • A public commitment on dataDeepL states that its Pro customers' texts are deleted after translation and are not used to train its models. We have no reason to doubt it.

What Corrext adds around it

Corrext is not one more engine. It is the environment in which an engine, DeepL Pro included, becomes usable on a matter covered by professional secrecy.

  • Where processing happens, decided by youStorage on Swiss servers in every case; processing exclusively in Switzerland with LexMachina or in Highly sensitive content mode, in German, French, Italian and English.
  • A warning at the right momentChoosing an engine hosted abroad displays your organisation's Attorney-Client privilege notice, in the tool, when the choice is made, not in a forgotten set of rules.
  • Swiss terminology, with sourcesThe CHnell concordancer shows the term in aligned segments from the Federal Gazette, fedlex.admin.ch or other official sources, with its domain.
  • The rest of the workAlternatives compared by engine, Light, Full or Double review ordered in the tool, quote and delivery time, certified, notarised or apostilled translation.

Eleven criteria, verifiable on both sides

What we say about DeepL Pro is limited to what is public and stable: a general-purpose translation engine, infrastructure located outside Switzerland, no integrated legal review chain, no Swiss law concordancer with sources. On everything else, DeepL Pro does what it says it does, and does it well.

An example involving the Banking Act

A banking law sentence, translated from French into German in Corrext. The two outputs below were collected on the same day, in the same alternatives panel.

Source · French

L'établissement assujetti veille au respect du secret bancaire au sens de l'art. 47 LB et met en œuvre les exigences de la FINMA en matière de lutte contre le blanchiment d'argent.

LexMachina

Das beaufsichtigte Institut sorgt für die Einhaltung des Bankgeheimnisses nach Artikel 47 BankG und setzt die Anforderungen der FINMA zur Bekämpfung der Geldwäscherei um.

DeepL Pro

Das unterstellte Institut sorgt für die Einhaltung des Bankgeheimnisses im Sinne von Art. 47 BankG und setzt die Anforderungen der FINMA zur Bekämpfung der Geldwäscherei um.

Both outputs are correct. Each cites Art. 47 BankG, the German abbreviation of the Banking Act, and uses the Swiss term Geldwäscherei rather than Geldwäsche. They differ on one point, which a legal translator will settle against the applicable German text, not by ear: how to render ‘établissement assujetti’ (the institution subject to supervision), beaufsichtigt in one case, unterstellt in the other.

Real outputs obtained in Corrext for this exact sentence, from French into German, in the Alternatives panel. This is a one-off, dated example, not a measurement: another sentence, another language pair or another generation would give a different result. That is exactly why Corrext shows the alternatives, each labelled with its engine, and lets you check each term in CHnell before deciding.

Should a law firm give up DeepL?

No, and banning it does not work: shadow translation starts again the next day, in a tab nobody sees. Corrext starts from the opposite premise. DeepL Pro remains available, alongside LexMachina, Azure OpenAI GPT and Claude, but choosing it applies your organisation's policy: pseudonymisation required, sensitive matters excluded, a notice shown at the moment of sending. Your teams keep the tool they like, and your professional secrecy keeps its framework.

Frequently asked questions on DeepL and legal translation

Can I use DeepL for a document covered by professional secrecy?

The issue is not the quality of the engine. It is the transfer of the document outside Switzerland. In Corrext, choosing DeepL Pro triggers the Attorney-Client privilege notice: your organisation requires the document to be fully pseudonymised, and prohibits these engines for sensitive matters. For a document that has not been pseudonymised, choose LexMachina or Highly sensitive content mode, which require processing exclusively in Switzerland. The rule that binds you remains that of your firm and your supervisory authority.

Does DeepL process my data in Switzerland?

No. DeepL is a German company and its infrastructure is located outside Switzerland. In Corrext, DeepL Pro is therefore classed among the third-party engines hosted abroad and triggers the pseudonymisation notice. For storage and processing exclusively in Switzerland, choose LexMachina or turn on Highly sensitive content mode, available in German, French, Italian and English.

Does Corrext replace DeepL?

No: Corrext can use DeepL Pro. You choose the engine each time you send a text, alongside LexMachina, Azure OpenAI GPT and Claude, and the Alternatives panel compares up to four suggestions, each labelled with its engine. What Corrext adds is not one more engine. It is the framework around it: Swiss storage, the notice before sending, the CHnell concordancer, human review ordered in the tool, the quote and certification.

What does an engine trained on Swiss law add?

LexMachina is trained on more than 15 million Swiss multilingual legal and financial segments. In practice, it knows the country's abbreviations and conventions: CO and OR, LB and BankG, LDIP and IPRG (the French and German abbreviations of the Code of Obligations, the Banking Act and the Federal Act on Private International Law), the Swiss spelling with ss, and the Federal Supreme Court rather than a foreign court. That does not make it infallible: no engine is a source of law, which is why Corrext shows the alternatives and the concordancer.

Does DeepL Pro keep my texts?

DeepL states publicly that texts translated by its Pro customers are deleted after translation and are not used to train its models. It is a serious commitment, and we have no reason to doubt it. For a matter covered by professional secrecy, the open question is not retention. It is the transfer of the document outside Switzerland, and the absence of any warning at the moment of sending.

How do I check a Swiss legal term after a translation?

In Corrext, highlight the word in the source text and click Fast Lookup. The CHnell concordancer displays aligned segments from official Swiss sources, with the domain and source of each occurrence, and a Show in context link. Verzugszins, for example, returns more than 300 results and Bankgeheimnis more than 400. This traceability is what a translation engine alone does not provide.
