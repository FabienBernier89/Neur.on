What general-purpose LLMs do remarkably well

They are the best tools lawyers have ever had for working on the meaning of a text. Saying so does not weaken our argument: it is the honest starting point for the comparison.

  • Rephrasing and simplifyingTurning a dense clause into a readable paragraph for a client without legal training, or the reverse, in the register requested.
  • Summarising and explainingBringing out the structure of a 50-page written submission, explaining a contractual mechanism, isolating each party's obligations.
  • Handling ambiguityOffering several readings of an ambiguous wording and spelling out what separates them, where a conventional translation engine silently picks one.
  • Exploring draftsQuickly producing several versions of a clause to feed an internal discussion, before settling on the version that will be negotiated.

What they cannot prove on their own

The problem is not the quality of the language: it is excellent. The problem is that none of their statements can be traced back to a source you can show to another lawyer or to an authority.

  • No sourced Swiss terminologyA term is rendered plausibly, with nothing to show how the Federal Gazette or fedlex.admin.ch actually renders it. In Corrext, that is the job of Fast lookup CHnell.
  • No guarantee on legal referencesAn article, an abbreviation or a court can be produced with full confidence and still be wrong, with no warning sign in the answer.
  • No review interfaceNo validation chain: no review level, no legal translator to order, no certification possible. In Corrext, review is ordered in Translation projects.
  • No quote, no delivery time, no governanceNothing to help you choose between understanding fast and having a text reviewed, and nothing to remind users of the firm's policy before a document is sent outside Switzerland.

An example of an error, and it is ours

A contract law clause translated from French into German in Corrext. Two alternatives from the same panel do not say the same thing, and one of them changes the meaning of the clause.

Source · French

Le débiteur en demeure doit des intérêts moratoires au taux de 5% l'an, conformément à l'art. 104 al. 1 CO, sans qu'une mise en demeure formelle soit nécessaire lorsqu'un terme a été convenu.

Faulty output · LexMachina

Der säumige Schuldner schuldet gemäss Art. 104 Abs. 1 OR Verzugszinsen in Höhe von 5% p.a., ohne dass eine förmliche Mahnung erforderlich wäre, wenn eine Kündigung vereinbart worden ist.

Alternative from the same panel · Azure OpenAI GPT

Der Schuldner im Verzug schuldet Verzugszinsen zum Satz von 5% pro Jahr gemäss Art. 104 Abs. 1 OR, ohne dass eine förmliche Mahnung erforderlich ist, wenn eine Frist vereinbart wurde.

Kündigung means termination of the contract, whereas the agreed ‘terme’ is a deadline, which the legal translator checks against the German text of the CO. The error comes from our own engine, not from a competitor. It illustrates exactly what we argue here: no engine, general-purpose or specialised, is a source of law. What protects the reader is not the promise of an infallible model. It is the alternatives shown side by side, the concordancer that leads back to the official source, and human review before anything is sent out.

Real outputs obtained in Corrext for this exact sentence, from French into German, in the Alternatives panel. This is a one-off, dated example, not a measurement: this type of error occurs with every engine, and another generation would give a different result. We kept it as it was rather than regenerating it, because it documents the real risk.

Frequently asked questions on general-purpose LLMs and Swiss law

Can I use ChatGPT to translate a contract?

To understand a clause, grasp its scope or prepare a discussion, yes: a general-purpose LLM does this very well. For a contract that will be signed, sent to a third party or filed with an authority, no, not on its own. It provides no sourced Swiss terminology, no guarantee on legal references, no review by a legal translator and no data processing in Switzerland. In Corrext, these models remain available, but the binding version goes through Light, Full or Double review.

How do I check a Swiss legal reference?

Do not ask the engine that has just produced it: ask a source. In Corrext, highlight the term or abbreviation in the source text and click Fast Lookup. The CHnell concordancer displays aligned segments from official Swiss sources, with the domain, the source and a Show in context link. You then see how the term is actually rendered in the Federal Gazette or on fedlex.admin.ch, not how a model assumes it should be.

Does Corrext use general-purpose LLMs?

Yes. Azure OpenAI GPT and Claude are available in Corrext, alongside LexMachina and DeepL Pro, and you choose the engine each time you send a text. Because they are hosted abroad, selecting them triggers your organisation's Attorney-Client privilege notice, which requires full pseudonymisation and prohibits these engines for sensitive matters. The Alternatives panel lets you compare their suggestions with the Swiss engine's on the same sentence.

Do general-purpose LLMs make up statutory provisions?

They can produce a reference, a court or a term that is plausible but wrong, and nothing in the answer flags the error. This risk is not specific to one provider: we documented it on our own engine. In a clause on default rendered in German, it used Kündigung, which means termination, where the agreed deadline was meant. That is precisely why Corrext displays several alternatives, each labelled with its engine, and gives access to the concordancer.

Are my conversations with a consumer AI assistant confidential?

Terms vary from one provider and one plan to another, and they change. Two points remain constant for a Swiss law firm: the document is transferred outside Switzerland, and nothing in the interface reminds users what their internal policy requires before they send it. In Corrext, the Attorney-Client privilege notice appears when the engine is chosen, and Highly sensitive content mode requires storage and processing exclusively in Switzerland.

When is a general-purpose LLM the right tool?

When you are working on meaning rather than on the final wording: summarising a long document, explaining a contractual mechanism, rephrasing a paragraph for a client without legal training, exploring several drafts of a clause. Corrext also offers its own rewriting function, Rephrasing, in five styles including Formal legal, Financial and Simplified.
