Italian to French legal translation. The third official language, in its own right

Ticino administers justice in Italian, and the Confederation publishes its acts in that language. Swiss legal Italian is not the Italian of Italy, and a general-purpose engine readily swaps one for the other.

This language pair in practice

Italian is one of the three languages in which federal acts are published and authoritative. Swiss legal terminology in Italian therefore comes from these texts, not from usage in Italy. The same words can exist on both sides of the border and designate different institutions and procedures.

An authoritative version

The Italian version of a federal act has the same force as the German and French versions. It is not a courtesy translation: it is a reference text.

Switzerland or Italy

Ricorso, precetto esecutivo and decreto d'accusa designate specific procedural acts in Switzerland. An engine trained on Italian law substitutes concepts from Italy that do not follow the same rules.The error is silent: the text still reads smoothly, but it describes a different procedure.

The institutions

The Tribunale federale (Federal Supreme Court) is the highest court in Switzerland, and the ricorso goes to it. A reference to an Italian court makes no sense in a Swiss document.

Abbreviations of their own

The Federal Act on Debt Enforcement and Bankruptcy is abbreviated LEF in Italian, LP in French, SchKG in German and DEBA in English. The Code of Obligations is CO in Italian, French and English, but OR in German.

Case law

A published decision is cited as DTF in Italian, ATF in French and BGE in German, with the same volume and page number. The Classified Compilation is abbreviated RS in Italian and in French, and SR in German.

Cantonal documents

The canton of Ticino's acts, rulings and forms follow the federal Italian terminology. That terminology is the foundation before any adaptation into French.

A native language

Italian is one of the four native languages of LexMachina, with German, French and English, and one of only four languages available in Highly sensitive content mode.For the foreign documents in a case file, the platform covers 30 languages in total.

Three case files from Ticino

Litigation. A Ticino ruling to work with

The procedure described is federal; only the language changes. Procedural terms are converted from the French version of the same article, not from a general Italian-French dictionary.

Debt enforcement. A precetto esecutivo to translate

Summons for payment (commandement de payer), objection (opposition), setting aside of the objection (mainlevée): the debt enforcement chain has fixed vocabulary in all three official languages. An approximation at this stage can mean a missed time limit, sometimes the loss of the right itself.

Companies. Ticino contracts to integrate

Commercial register extracts, articles of association and contracts are translated with the terms of the Code of Obligations. Registered company names and legal forms stay as they are.

Frequently asked questions about Italian to French translation

Is Swiss legal Italian different from the Italian of Italy?

Yes, and the difference concerns the essentials: institutions and procedures. A Swiss ricorso goes to the Tribunale federale. The precetto esecutivo belongs to the debt enforcement procedure under federal law, and the decreto d'accusa is an act under the Swiss Criminal Procedure Code. The same words sometimes exist in Italy and mean something else.

Is the Italian version of a federal act authoritative?

Yes, on the same footing as the German and French versions. That is why terminological equivalents can be read directly in the official texts. The three versions of an article give the three exact terms, without relying on a common-usage translation.

How are abbreviations of Acts converted?

According to the target language. The Federal Act on Debt Enforcement and Bankruptcy is abbreviated LEF in Italian and LP in French. The Federal Act on Private International Law is LDIP in both languages, but IPRG in German. The Code of Obligations stays CO in Italian and French. In English, the Confederation's translations use DEBA, PILA and CO. When in doubt, the full title of the Act is written out.

Do you also translate from French into Italian?

Yes. The four native languages work in every combination, in both directions. They are also the only languages available in Highly sensitive content mode; for foreign documents, the platform covers 30 languages in total.

Ticino documents often arrive scanned. Is that a problem?

No. The PDF to Word tab in Text and document translation converts a scan into an editable Word document before translation, so you keep the structure and can annotate afterwards. Files go back out in their original format.
