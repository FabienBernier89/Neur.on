Three situations where a certified extract is required

{{ICONE:societe}}Fiduciary firms. A subsidiary or an account abroad

As a rule, the foreign authority or bank requires the Swiss company's extract translated into its language, notarised and apostilled if the State is party to the Hague Convention. You order the Notarized & Apostilled Certification level online, with the paper copy sent by post or courier.

{{ICONE:loupe}}Legal departments. Due diligence abroad

A certified extract in English, or in the counterparty's language, usually goes with the file. Depending on the recipient, Corrext certification is enough or notarised certification is required. You choose the level at the second step, without changing provider.

{{ICONE:institution}}Banks. Documenting a company for use abroad

In a KYC review by an institution or a correspondent bank abroad, the Swiss extract must generally be provided translated and certified. The level required depends on the counterparty: Corrext certification for a private third party, notarised or apostilled if an authority is involved.

Which certification level for which use?

As a rule, Corrext certification is enough for internal use or a private third party. Swiss authorities and courts require notarised certification. Foreign authorities of a State party to the Hague Convention require notarised and apostilled certification. In every case, the receiving authority decides on the level required: check with it before you order.

Frequently asked questions about certified extracts

What is an apostille?

The apostille is the formality provided for by the Hague Convention of 5 October 1961. It authenticates the signature of the notary who certified the translation, so that the document is recognised in the other States party to the Convention. On auszug-hr.ch, it is issued by the cantonal State Chancellery in the Notarized & Apostilled Certification level.

What certification level does a foreign authority require?

It depends on the authority and the country. As a rule, an authority of a State party to the Hague Convention requires a notarised and apostilled translation. A Swiss authority is usually satisfied with notarised certification. For internal use or a private third party, Corrext certification is often accepted. Check with the receiving authority before you order: it sets the requirement.

How long does a certified translation take?

The delivery time depends on the certification level, the target language and the option you choose: standard or express, for Switzerland or international. You choose it at the fourth step of the order, and it is shown before you confirm. Notarised or apostilled certification adds a step with the notary, then with the State Chancellery.
