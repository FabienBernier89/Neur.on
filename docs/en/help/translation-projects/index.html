Translation projects

Translation projects takes you through three ordering steps (project set-up, file overview and quotes), then terminology, engine choice and review in the editor. Here is how to create a project, set your terms, review the translation and approve it.

How do I create a translation project?

Translation projects runs in three steps shown at the top of the screen: ‘Set-up’, ‘File overview’ and then ‘Quotes’. The first step asks for a project name, a confidentiality level and one or more target languages. The ‘Next’ button stays inactive until you choose a target language. The project is created as soon as you move to the next step.

  1. Open Translation projects from the home page: the Set-up step appears first.
  2. Enter a name in Project name, or leave the ‘Use default name’ toggle on: it builds a name from the date, the organisation and the user.
  3. Answer the question ‘How confidential are the project and the uploaded files?’ by choosing one of the two confidentiality cards.
  4. Click Select target languages and tick your target languages in the list, using the search field if needed.
  5. Check the language tags shown below the button: you can remove each one by clicking its cross.
  6. Click Next, which becomes active as soon as you select a target language.
Good to know
  • The project is created when you move to the ‘File overview’ step: it then has its own address.
  • You can select several target languages for the same project: the quote then shows them file by file.
  • The settings button at the bottom of the workflow displayed nothing during the audit of the app: its function is not documented here.
What does the Set-up step ask me for?

The project name, the target languages and the confidentiality level, then two further questions: one related to insider dealing, the other about translating the comments in the document.

Good to know
  • The target languages cannot be changed once the project has been created: check them before you move to the next step.
  • The confidentiality level applies to every file in the project.
Which confidentiality level should I choose for a project?

At the ‘Set-up’ step, Corrext asks you to choose between two cards. ‘Standard confidentiality’ saves the project on Corrext's Swiss servers and processes it in or outside Switzerland, depending on the engine you choose. The project can then serve as a translation memory, for you alone. ‘Highly sensitive content’ stores and processes exclusively in Switzerland, with no reuse in memory, for German, French, Italian and English.

  1. At the Set-up step, find the question ‘How confidential are the project and the uploaded files?’
  2. Choose Standard confidentiality for an everyday project: storage on Corrext's Swiss servers, processing according to the engine you choose.
  3. Choose Highly sensitive content for a confidential matter: storage and processing exclusively in Switzerland.
  4. Check your languages: in sensitive mode, only EN, FR, IT and DE are available.
  5. Continue through the workflow: your choice applies to all the files uploaded to the project.
Good to know
  • With standard confidentiality, projects are kept on Corrext's Swiss servers and can be reused as translation memory by you alone.
  • In sensitive mode, no translated content feeds a translation memory for future projects.
  • The confidentiality setting does not replace care over the engine: a third-party engine hosted abroad triggers the Attorney-Client privilege notice.
How do I upload files and check the detected language and category?

The second step, ‘File overview’, accepts your documents in .pdf, .doc, .docx, .xls, .xlsx, .ppt and .pptx format, up to 50 MB each. Each uploaded file shows its name, its number of standard pages, its detected language and its detected legal category, for example 2.5 standard pages for a short document. You can change the language and the category: check them, because the quote is based on them.

  1. At the File overview step, click the upload area or drag your documents onto it.
  2. Prefer the original format, .docx or .xlsx rather than PDF, as the upload area advises.
  3. Check the number of standard pages calculated for each file: it is the unit for the quote and the delivery time.
  4. Check Detected language and correct it if the detection was wrong.
  5. Check Detected category, the detected legal category, and adjust it if it does not match the document.
  6. Remove a file with its delete icon, or download it with the icon next to it, then click ‘Next’.
Good to know
  • The ‘Next’ button stays locked until you upload a file: the quotes step needs at least one document.
  • The detected legal category guides the processing: correcting it at this step avoids a quote based on the wrong area of law.
  • A scanned PDF should first go through the ‘PDF to Word’ tab of Text and document translation, and then be uploaded in Word format.
How is a standard page calculated?

A standard page has 1,800 characters, including spaces. Corrext counts the standard pages of each file at the File overview step. This unit, together with the detected legal category, is used for the quote and the delivery time.

Good to know
  • Two short documents can count fewer standard pages than a single dense document.
Internal review or custom workflow: which should I choose?

The third step, ‘Quotes’, opens on two tab cards. ‘Internal review’ estimates the review time you will do yourselves and ends with the ‘Create project’ button. ‘Custom workflow’ adds an external review: you set a due date, an order of priority and, for each file, an internal or external review type and a level. The button then becomes ‘Generate quote’.

  1. At the Quotes step, choose the Internal review card if you review the machine translation yourself.
  2. Fill in the ‘Internal review specifications’ block: the number of standard pages to review and the price per standard page you charge.
  3. Read the ‘Estimated time’ box, which sums up the estimated review time, then click Create project.
  4. Or choose the Custom workflow card to entrust the review to a legal translator or an agency.
  5. Set a due date in ‘Due date’: the calendar greys out weekends and an estimated review time appears.
  6. Answer the question on the order of priority for processing the files, then set up the file table: the File, Languages, Review and Review level columns.
  7. Click Generate quote, which becomes active once you have entered a due date and a review level.
Good to know
  • The price per standard page field in the internal review is used to value your own work in the estimate: the figure is yours to set.
  • In the file table, the ‘Review’ column distinguishes ‘External’ and ‘Internal’: a single project can mix both.
  • The ‘Contact us for an earlier delivery date’ link lets you request an earlier due date than the one offered.
What do the three levels of external review cover?

Corrext offers three levels of external review. ‘Light review’ corrects major errors, omissions and mistranslations, so that you can understand the document and circulate it internally. ‘Full review’ adds consistency, idiomatic usage, style and tone, for a document intended for third parties. ‘Double review’ has the translation reviewed by two legal translators and adds a certificate of accuracy.

  1. At the ‘Quotes’ step, switch to the Custom workflow card and scroll down to the file table.
  2. Open the Review level column for the file concerned: the three levels are offered there.
  3. Choose Light review, carried out by a legal translator, for a document intended for understanding and internal circulation.
  4. Choose Full review, also carried out by a legal translator, for a document to be shared with third parties.
  5. Choose Double review & certified translation, carried out by a specialised agency, for legal use, publication or filing with an authority.
  6. Hover over the information icon next to a level to read its exact definition in the app.
Good to know
  • The ‘Double review’ certificate is signed by the specialised translation agency in charge of the project. It attests to the accuracy and impartiality of the translation.
  • You choose the level file by file: a project can combine a ‘Light review’ of exhibits and a ‘Full review’ of a contract.
  • For a commercial register extract to be filed with an authority, use the dedicated certified translation service instead.
How do I get a quote and a delivery time?

At the ‘Quotes’ step, Corrext calculates an estimate from the number of standard pages in your files and the detected legal category. An ‘Estimated time’ box breaks down the preparation time, the internal review time and the external review time, and shows a total cost excluding tax. The ‘Generate quote’ button produces the quote once you have entered the due date and the review level.

  1. First check, at the ‘File overview’ step, the number of standard pages and the detected category of each file.
  2. At the Quotes step, choose the card that matches your needs: ‘Internal review’ or ‘Custom workflow’.
  3. Enter the due date in ‘Due date’ and the review level of each file: the estimate updates.
  4. Read the Estimated time box: it shows preparation, internal review and external review separately.
  5. Check the total cost excluding tax shown below the estimate before you go any further.
  6. Click Generate quote for the quote of a custom workflow, or Create project for an internal review.
Good to know
  • The unit of calculation is the standard page, not the file: two short documents can count less than a single dense document.
  • The cost shown excludes tax. Corrext states this explicitly in the estimate box.
  • ‘Generate quote’ sends the request to the agency in charge of the external review: only click it when your settings are final.
How do I set the terminology for a project?

Once the project has been created, the ‘Project terminology’ screen shows the terms Corrext has identified in your documents, for example ‘résiliation’ (termination) or ‘for exclusif’ (exclusive jurisdiction). Clicking a term opens a field where you enter its translation in each target language. You can also add your own terms with ‘Custom term’. The project's machine translation uses the translations you set here exactly as they are.

  1. Click Create project at the ‘Quotes’ step: the Project terminology screen opens.
  2. Browse the suggested terms, grouped under the tab of the source language.
  3. Click a term, for example ‘résiliation’ (termination), to add it to the project terminology.
  4. Enter its translation in the field of each target language, for example ‘EN’.
  5. Add a term that is not in the list with Custom term, or remove one by clicking the bin icon.
  6. Click Next to move on to the choice of engine.
Good to know
  • The translation you set applies to the exact form of the term. A related word, such as the verb ‘résilier’ (to terminate), is not affected.
  • The suggested terms come from your own documents: the list changes from one project to another.
  • For terminology shared by all projects, your administrator can import a termbase in TBX or XML format at organisation level.
How do I choose the engine for a project?

After the terminology, the ‘Choose a machine translation engine’ screen shows the engines as cards. The recommended Swiss engine is preselected. LexMachina, Neur.on's engine, ensures 100% Swiss processing. Third-party engines display a reminder: your internal policy may require pseudonymisation first. Clicking ‘Next’ starts the machine translation and opens the project dashboard.

  1. From ‘Project terminology’, click ‘Next’: the Choose a machine translation engine screen appears.
  2. Find the card marked Recommended: it is the preselected Swiss engine.
  3. Read the description on each card: the LexMachina card states ‘100% Swiss Data Processing’.
  4. For a third-party engine (‘DeepL Pro’, ‘Azure OpenAI GPT’ or ‘Claude’), read the reminder about pseudonymisation before you choose it.
  5. Click Next: the translation starts and the project dashboard opens.
Good to know
  • Your administrator can restrict the list of engines allowed for your organisation.
  • In ‘Highly sensitive content’ mode, only LexMachina, the Swiss engine, remains available.
  • According to its card, LexMachina, the recommended engine, stays closest to the source text.
How do I work on a project with colleagues?

The project dashboard has a Team block, which manages the people working on the project. In the editor, the ‘Sync’ tab of the side panel then brings everyone's work together: version conflicts, incoming changes from a colleague and propagation decisions on repeated segments.

Good to know
  • Propagation decisions apply a correction to identical segments, segment by segment, once you have made your choice.
