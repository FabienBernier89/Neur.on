Translating company documents, in practice

Translating in corporate law means carrying over the documents that record the life of a legal entity: articles of association, organisational regulations, notices of meeting, minutes, commercial register entries. Corporate bodies and legal forms have a precise name in each of the three official languages. That name is the one in the register, not a free translation.

Articles of association follow the provisions of the CO almost word for word. The translation keeps the official wording of each concept, from the objects of the company to the share capital.

The acts company documents cite every day

Swiss company law sits in the Code of Obligations, supplemented by the ordinance that organises the commercial register. Two other acts come into play as soon as an entity changes its legal form or a foundation is involved.

CO
Code of Obligations

Titles 23 to 32 govern the simple partnership, the general partnership, the limited partnership, the company limited by shares, the partnership limited by shares, the limited liability company and the cooperative. They also cover the commercial register, business names and commercial accounting; OR in German, CO in French and Italian.

HRegV
Commercial Register Ordinance

It sets the content of entries, applications and extracts. Fedlex has no English translation: the English title is descriptive. HRegV in German, ORC in French.

FusG
Mergers Act

It governs any change in the structure of a company or a foundation. Fedlex has no English translation: the English version of the CO calls it the Mergers Act. FusG in German, LFus in French.

CC
Swiss Civil Code

It governs associations and foundations, which the Code of Obligations does not cover; ZGB in German, CC in French and Italian.

Three moments in a company's life that go through translation

Incorporation. Articles of association in the canton's language

The founder drafts in English; the commercial register office expects German or French. The translation uses the wording of the CO, and any doubtful term can be checked in context in CHnell before filing.

General meeting. Minutes to circulate

The minutes are kept in one language and read in three. The document comes back in its layout, with quorum and majority statements, and a Light review is usually enough for internal circulation.

Governance. Regulations for every subsidiary

The same regulations apply to entities in German, French and Italian-speaking Switzerland. CHnell's shared terminology resources keep the vocabulary identical from one version to the next, year after year.

Frequently asked questions on company documents

Do articles of association have to be translated into all three official languages?

No. The law does not require three versions: registration takes place in an official language of the canton. Companies with shareholders or subsidiaries in several language regions do circulate a working translation, however, and that translation must use the register's labels exactly.

How do I get a certified translation of a commercial register extract?

You order it online at auszug-hr.ch, the Corrext service accessible from the platform's home page. There are three certification levels, depending on the intended use: a certificate signed and stamped by Corrext, authentication by a Swiss notary, and then an apostille from the cantonal State Chancellery for use abroad.

Can confidential minutes be translated safely?

Yes, provided you choose the right mode. Storage is in Switzerland in every case. Highly sensitive content mode also requires processing in Switzerland only, with no reuse of the content in a translation memory. Engines hosted abroad trigger a warning before anything is sent.

How do I keep the same vocabulary from one year to the next?

With the translation memory and the concordancer. In Standard confidentiality, a project feeds a memory that only your organisation can reuse. Fast lookup CHnell also accepts your own terminology resources, on top of its 30 sourced domains. The resources can be shared across your teams, so no subsidiary ends up with a different label.

Which languages are covered for company documents?

German, French, Italian and English are native, in every pair, and the platform covers 30 languages in total. This helps when a foreign shareholder asks for a working version of the articles of association or of the minutes. Between them, the four native languages cover the German, French and Italian-speaking regions of Switzerland.

Can my own legal team do the review?

Yes. The Internal review mode in the project workflow organises review by your teams and estimates the time needed from the number of standard pages detected in the uploaded files. External review by a legal translator remains available alongside.
