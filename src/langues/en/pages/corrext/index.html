Four tools, one control room

Each tool has its own page. Together, they cover the translation process, from checking a term to certified translation.

Text and document translation. Text or file, translated on the spot

Paste a text or upload up to 25 Word, Excel, PowerPoint or PDF files. Choose the engine and target language, and compare up to four alternatives, each labelled with its engine. Check a term in CHnell by simply highlighting it. Rephrase in five styles.

  • Text up to 10,000 characters, files up to 50 MB, PDF to Word conversion
  • LexMachina, DeepL Pro, Azure OpenAI GPT or Claude, chosen for each upload
  • Highly sensitive content mode: processing in Switzerland only
Explore Text and document translation

Translation projects. From request to quote in three steps

Name the project, choose its confidentiality level (Standard or Highly sensitive) and upload the files. Corrext detects the language, the legal category and the number of standard pages. It then calculates the delivery time for an internal or external review, at three levels.

  • Internal review by your teams, or external review by a legal translator or a certified agency
  • Instant quote and delivery time, due date, file priority
  • Standard projects reused as private memory; no reuse in Highly sensitive mode
  • Sentence-by-sentence review in the review editor, with your corrections added to your memory

Fast lookup CHnell. Every term checked in its official context

CHnell, the Neur.on concordancer, aligns source and target segments from the Federal Gazette, Fedlex, FINMA and the federal administration. Thirty domains, resources that are shared or your organisation's own, and precise search operators.

  • Verzugszins ↔ intérêt moratoire: 324 aligned segments, each with its source
  • Filters by domain, source and resource type; operators "" - or
  • Fast Lookup from any translation, with a simple highlight

Commercial register extracts. Translated, certified, apostilled, online

Search for the company by name or by UID (the Swiss business identification number), then choose the certification level, delivery method and delivery time. The translation is done by legal translators who specialise in corporate housekeeping, assisted by language models specifically trained on Swiss commercial register extracts.

  • Three levels: Corrext certification, notarised, notarised and apostilled (Hague Convention)
  • Over 30 target languages, delivery by email or post, standard or express
  • Fully online ordering process, ISO 27001-certified Swiss infrastructure

Frequently asked questions about Corrext

What is Corrext?

Corrext is Neur.on's legal and financial translation platform. It brings four tools together in a single Swiss environment: Text and document translation, Translation projects, Fast lookup CHnell and certified commercial register extracts.

Where are my documents stored and processed?

Corrext is hosted in Switzerland, with Infomaniak. Documents are processed in Switzerland in Highly sensitive content mode or with the LexMachina engine. Engines hosted abroad trigger your organisation's confidentiality notice and are not available in Highly sensitive content mode. Neur.on is ISO 27001-certified.

Which translation engines can I use?

LexMachina, DeepL Pro, Azure OpenAI GPT or Claude, chosen for each upload. Corrext can show up to four alternatives, each labelled with its engine, so you can compare before you choose.

Which formats and languages does Corrext accept?

Seven types of office files, from PDF to PowerPoint, up to 25 files of 50 MB per upload, and pasted text up to 10,000 characters. German, French, Italian and English are native, and the platform offers 30 languages in all.

Does Corrext replace review by a translator?

No. For a binding document, you order a review in Translation projects: Light review or Full review by a legal translator, or Double review & certified translation. The quote and delivery time appear before you confirm anything.

How does Corrext integrate with our tools?

Through a native MS Word plug-in or a browser application, through a REST API for your DMS and intranets, or as an on-premises deployment. Access rights can be set down to document level, via SharePoint or Active Directory.
