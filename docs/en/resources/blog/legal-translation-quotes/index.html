Standard pages, review levels, delivery times: understanding a legal translation quote

Good practice Neur.on legal and language team 15 September 2026 10 min read
Share
In brief

A legal translation quote fits in four or five lines, and each line stands for a decision of substance. Here is what each line means, what each review level guarantees, and what a quote never tells you: the time your legal experts will spend on it.

Two quotes for the same contract can differ by a factor of three without either being abusive. They do not count the same thing, they do not promise the same work and they do not carry the same risk. Reading a quote means knowing what you are buying: a text to understand, a text to pass on, or a text that will hold up against third parties.

1. Why the unit is not a page of paper

The counting unit of a translation quote is the standard page. It is not a sheet of your document: it is a conventional quantity of text. A written submission of ten widely spaced sheets, with headings and white space, can amount to fewer standard pages than a three-sheet contract set densely in 9-point type.

This unit exists for a simple reason: it is the only one that stays stable when the format changes. The same content uploaded as a Word file, a scanned PDF or a slide presentation gives a different number of sheets and the same number of standard pages. This is therefore the basis for calculating both the price and the delivery time.

The difficulty is that the precise definition of a standard page varies from one provider to another, in characters or in words, with or without spaces. It is the first thing to ask for in writing, before you even compare two amounts: without this definition, the comparison is meaningless.

The useful step. Ask each provider how many standard pages they count for the same file. If the two figures differ, you know where the gap comes from before you discuss anything else.

2. What the machine detects before pricing

On a modern translation platform, the quote is no longer produced after a person has read the document: it is calculated on upload, from what the system recognises. In Corrext, uploading a file displays three pieces of information, all editable: the number of standard pages, the detected language and the detected legal category of the document.

The category is not an administrative detail. It determines the reference terminology, the profile of the reviewer to involve and, as a result, the processing time. A private insurance contract, a prudential circular and an arbitration agreement are not reviewed with the same eye or at the same speed.

Always check this detection. A miscategorised document produces a quote that is right in form and wrong in substance, because it calls on the wrong expertise. It is one of the few points where thirty seconds of attention change the result.

3. The three levels of external review, word for word

Review levels are not quality tiers in the commercial sense: they are different scopes of work. Below are the exact definitions shown in Corrext, in English as in the application, followed by what they mean in practice.

Light review · Legal translator

Correcting what is wrong

“The output of the machine translation is reviewed by a translator to correct any significant errors, omissions or mistranslations. The result is a complete and semantically accurate translation of the document, recommended for understanding and internal transmission.”

A translator goes over the machine output to remove significant errors, omissions and mistranslations. You get a text that is complete and accurate in substance. What is not guaranteed: stylistic consistency, idiomatic wording, a steady register. This is the right level to understand a case file, prepare a meeting or circulate a text internally.

Full review · Legal translator

Making the text fit to share

“The output of the machine translation is reviewed by a translator, adapted and modified, with additional focus on consistency, idiomatic wording, style and tone. This ensures a good legal translation of your documents, ready to be shared with third parties.”

The translator goes beyond correction: they adapt, harmonise and work on consistency, idiomatic wording, style and tone. The difference from the previous level is not extra elegance. It is that the document can leave your organisation without exposing you. This is the level expected for a document addressed to an opposing party, a client or a partner.

Double review & certified translation

Taking on responsibility

“Full review of the documents by two qualified legal translators. A certificate signed by the specialized translation agency in charge of the project attests to the accuracy and impartiality of the translation. Suitable for documents intended for legal use, publication, or submission to authorities.”

Two qualified legal translators review the whole text, and a specialised agency signs a certificate attesting to the accuracy and impartiality of the translation. What changes here is not only the number of pairs of eyes: a third party attests in writing. This is the level required for legal use, publication or submission to an authority.

Remember the progression: the first level corrects, the second harmonises, the third attests. Choosing the level comes down to a single question: who will read this document, and what happens if it contains an error?

4. Internal review: free in appearance, paid for in legal experts' time

Alongside external review, most organisations practise internal review: someone at the law firm or in the legal department reviews the translation themselves. On a quote, this line shows zero. That is exactly where the cost is hidden.

Corrext handles this point in an unusual and instructive way. In the internal review specifications, the application asks you to enter the price per standard page that you yourself charge for this review, together with an hourly estimate. It then shows a total cost that includes your own work. As long as you have entered nothing, this total stays at zero: the tool shows, quite literally, that internal review looks free because no one has put a value on it.

The time estimate, on the other hand, appears straight away. For a document of 2.5 standard pages observed in the application, internal review was estimated at one to two hours. Apply this order of magnitude to the real volume of a case and to the person who will review it. The question is no longer whether external review is expensive, but whether it costs more than the hours it frees up.

Do this once and for all. Set an internal hourly rate for review for each reviewer profile. Without it, every trade-off between internal and external review is made by intuition, and intuition always chooses what does not appear on an invoice.

5. Delivery time is not an independent variable

Delivery time is derived from the same data as the price: the number of standard pages, the document category, the review level chosen and the language combination. It is therefore not negotiated separately. A quote that announces a date without asking for this information announces an intention, not a commitment.

Two mechanisms deserve attention in an online quote workflow.

  • The ‘Due date’ is an input, not an output. You enter when you want the document; the system calculates the corresponding review time and tells you whether the date can be met. In Corrext, weekends are greyed out in the calendar, and a link lets you get in touch for faster delivery than the calculation allows.
  • Delivery time breaks down into stages. On a project observed in the application, the estimate distinguished a preparation phase, internal review and external review, each estimated separately. This breakdown is useful: it shows where the time goes, and therefore what a trade-off can really shorten.

One last point, often forgotten: when a batch contains several files, the processing order is a decision to take. If three documents out of twelve are critical for your hearing, say so when you request the quote; a serious workflow will explicitly ask whether you want to set an order of priority.

6. Certification, notarisation, apostille: a separate case

Some documents need more than a reviewed translation: they need a certified translation, sometimes notarised, sometimes with an apostille. These three levels are not degrees of care. They are different formalities, which involve different third parties.

  • Certification: the translation comes with a signed and stamped certificate attesting to its accuracy.
  • Notarisation: a Swiss notary is also involved, to authenticate the signature on that certificate.
  • Apostille: the cantonal State Chancellery adds the apostille provided for by the Hague Convention of 5 October 1961, so that the document has effect abroad.

Delivery time and workflow change at each stage, because they depend on outside parties. Establish the required level before launching the translation, by asking the authority or the receiving party. Redoing a certification afterwards means starting the end of the chain again.

7. Eight questions to ask before accepting a quote

  1. What is your definition of a standard page? In characters or in words, with or without spaces.
  2. What exactly does the proposed review level guarantee? Ask for the written definition, not the sales adjective.
  3. Who reviews, and with what specialisation? A legal translator, a lawyer-linguist, an agency: depending on the area of law, these are not the same thing.
  4. What happens if there is a terminology disagreement? Who decides, on what source, and how quickly.
  5. Where is the data stored, and where is it processed? The two answers are distinct, and the second depends on the engine used.
  6. Does the content feed a translation memory? If so, which one, who can access it, and can you switch it off for a sensitive matter.
  7. What does the quote cover if the source text changes along the way? A change to the original document after the start is the main cause of overruns.
  8. In what form is the deliverable returned? Original format preserved, bilingual document, working file: this determines how much rework falls to you.

In Corrext, these points are not negotiated by email. The Translation projects workflow runs in three steps (Set-up, File overview, Quotes), shows the number of standard pages and the detected category on upload, then produces a quote and a delivery time from the review level and the ‘Due date’ chosen, file by file. The four quality levels and the certified commercial register extracts service cover the rest of the range, up to the apostille.

Frequently asked questions

What is a standard page in translation?

A standard page is a conventional counting unit based on the volume of text, not on the number of sheets in the document. A file of ten widely spaced sheets can amount to fewer standard pages than a dense three-sheet contract. The exact definition, in characters or words, varies from one provider to another: ask for it in writing, because it is what makes two quotes comparable.

Why does a quote ask for the legal category of the document?

Because the category determines the reference terminology, the profile of the reviewer and therefore the processing time. In Corrext, the application detects the language and category of the document on upload, for example Private Insurance, and the user can correct this detection before the quote is calculated.

What is the difference between Light review, Full review and Double review?

Light review corrects significant errors, omissions and mistranslations: the result is complete and accurate in substance, recommended for understanding and internal circulation. Full review also works on consistency, idiomatic wording, style and tone, for a document intended for third parties. Double review & certified translation has the whole text reviewed by two qualified legal translators, with a certificate attesting to its accuracy and impartiality, for legal use, publication or submission to an authority.

Is internal review free?

The provider does not invoice it, but it takes up the time of your legal experts. This is why Corrext asks you, in the internal review specifications, to enter the price per standard page that you yourself charge for this review. The quote then shows a total cost that includes your own work, instead of leaving it invisible.

What should I ask before accepting a legal translation quote?

Ask for the definition of the standard page, what exactly the chosen review level guarantees, who reviews and with what specialisation, what happens if there is a terminology disagreement, where the data is stored and processed, whether the content feeds a translation memory, what is covered if the source text changes after the start, and in what form the deliverable is returned.
