Guides. In-depth methods you can apply as they are

Longer than our articles and organised in steps, these guides answer a question of organisation, not of terminology. You read them once, apply them over a quarter, and they require no IT project.

What you will find in this collection

A guide answers a question that an article cannot cover: how to make a practice last over time. It describes a sequence of steps, a division of roles, written rules and indicators, in an order that has been tested.

Each guide is written to be applied without us. You can draw your own page of rules, glossary and schedule from it without buying anything: that is the point. Our tools only appear at the end, where they answer a specific step.

Organisation

Organising legal translation in a Swiss law firm

In a law firm, translation is rarely a process: it is a sum of individual arrangements, invisible in the budget and yet binding on the firm. This guide makes it visible, divides it between three roles, frames it with a one-page set of rules and measures it with six indicators.

  • A four-week survey to establish the real volume
  • Three families of need: understand, produce, file
  • Who decides the review level, and who carries it out
  • How to stop shadow translation without banning anything

Multilingual project

Preparing a multilingual annual report

An annual report in several languages is not a document translated several times. It is a single document that must say the same thing everywhere, on the same date, with the same figures. This guide starts from publication and works back, milestone by milestone.

  • The backward schedule and its bottlenecks
  • The source text freeze: why it holds or fails
  • Consistency of terminology, figures, structure and tone
  • The Swiss formats to check before publication
