in brief

Knowing where your documents are stored means almost nothing. The question that really undertakes professional secrecy is where the text is read and calculated, motor by motor, sending by sending. How to ask this question and how to interpret the answer.

The lawyer's professional secrecy is governed by federal law and its application to digital tools is not governed by an internal note. It is settled by verifiable facts: where the text rests, where it is calculated, who accesses it, which is retained. This article does not say what is permitted; it says what needs to be known before it is decided.

Storage is not processed

It is the distinction that most trade documentaries have in the dark, and it is the one that decides everything.

QuestionWhat it coversWhat it does not guarantee
Where are my files stored? The physical location where the document is permanently based: data center, country, infrastructure operator. The content has never left this country during its processing.
Where are my files processed? The place where the text is actually read and calculated by an engine, even for a few seconds. Keep the file in the same place afterwards.

A platform can easily host your documents on Swiss servers and transmit each segment to an engine hosted elsewhere to translate it. Both claims are true at the same time, and only the second account for a secret file. Transitional processing remains processing: the text has been transmitted, read, calculated and returned outside your jurisdiction.

Therefore, the wording to be required from a supplier is never “your data is in Switzerland”, but a sentence separating the two: Storage on servers in Switzerland in all cases; treatment in Switzerland in this configuration, abroad in another. If your point of contact cannot answer separately, they cannot answer.

What happens when you choose an engine hosted abroad

Professional translation platforms are currently multi-engine: they orchestrate several models, some of which are operated by third parties outside of Switzerland. This is good, because the engines do not have the same forces, and it is a point of vigilance, because the treatment location changes with the selected engine, on each shipment.

Three things happen, which must be borne in mind.

  • the text is outside the national perimeter. It is transmitted to an operator subject to another right, in a country whose authorities may have different access powers from yours.
  • the question of reuse arises. an engine can store the submitted content, use it to improve its models, or log it. The conditions vary depending on the contract, the offer and the contracting entity: this is a written question for each engine, not a hypothesis.
  • the subcontracting chain is extending. between you and the engine, there may be an integrator, host, infrastructure provider. Each additional link is another access point to document.

Conversely, an engine operated in the same country as storage removes this breakage. This is the logic of the strictest mode of a platform: in Corrext, option higher sensitive content requires storage and processing exclusively in Switzerland for German, French, Italian and English, and, depending on the definition displayed by the application, translated content is not reused in a translation memory for future projects.

Pseudonymisation: what it protects, what it does not protect

Pseudonymising a document before sending it to a foreign engine means removing or replacing items identifying persons and the case: names of the parties, companies, advisors, addresses, case references, and sometimes the very subject matter of the dispute.

This is a serious measure of prudence. It has two limitations that must be clearly stated.

  • It is not an anonymisation. the link to the person remains with you, and the text remains in the folder. The risk of disclosure is reduced, not eliminated.
  • the context reidentifies. a singular transaction, an unusual clause, a combination of dates, jurisdictions, and sectors may be sufficient to recognize a known case, even without a single name of its own. The larger the file, the less protection pseudonymisation offers.

Practical costs must also be taken into account: pseudonymisation correctly forty handpieces takes time, and partial pseudonymisation gives false security that is worse than pseudonymisation at all, because it lowers vigilance. for truly sensitive files, the right answer is not better to blanket out: it's not to leave the country.

sorting rule in one sentence. If the only measure that makes a consignment acceptable is pseudonymisation, ask yourself what the risk would be if it were imperfect. If the answer is “much,” choose treatment that does not leave Switzerland.

Why a warning in the tool is better than a Charter

Most organizations have a policy for the use of digital tools. It is signed on arrival, stored in an intranet, and forgotten on the day an employee has to understand forty documents in German before the hearing the next day. It's not a disciplinary problem: it's a time issue.

a rule is only applied if it appears at the time of the decision to the person making it. This is exactly what a contextual warning does. In Corrext, selecting an engine hosted abroad displays the following notice, as displayed in the App:

Attorney-Client privilege Sheet
“You have chosen to use third party translation engines Hosted broad. Before uploading your file (s), your Organization requires you to remove any references that could tactially disclose the identity of Clients, any other related parties, or the subject matter at hand. Third-party translation engines hosted broad May exclusively be used for documents that have been pletely pseudonymized. The use of such engines for high-profile or price-sensitive matters is briefly prohibited.”

Three things deserve to be noted in this operative part, regardless of the tool.

  • Notice is triggered by an act, not by a calendar. It appears only when the risk appears, keeping it alert.
  • It sets forth the policy of the organization, not that of the supplier. It is the cabinet that decides what is prohibited; the tool merely recalls it where it is useful.
  • It does not block. the user remains responsible and may knowingly skip. A tool that prohibits everything ends up being circumvented by public solutions, which do not warn anyone of anything.

This is the most important point for a Directorate of legal Affairs. The main risk is not that an employee chooses a foreign engine in a managed tool: this is because he glues a paragraph into a free online service because the approved tool told him no. a practicable policy reduces the risk more than a perfect policy.

Twelve questions to ask a supplier

Location

  1. Where are the storage servers, in which country, and who operates them?
  2. Where is the treatment carried out for each engine offered?
  3. Is there a configuration in which the processing never comes out of the country, and how does the user trigger it?
  4. To what right is the contract subject, and in which jurisdiction?

Speakers

  1. Which subcontractors are involved in the chain, and which subcontractors access the content?
  2. Who at the supplier can technically read a document, and is this consultation logged?
  3. Are human relectors employed or self-employed, and by what commitment of confidentiality are they linked?
  4. Is a notification provided for in the case of an application for access by an authority, insofar as permitted by applicable law?

Data and evidence

  1. What data is reused, for what purpose, and can it be disable by project?
  2. How long are content stored, and how is it deleted?
  3. Which certification is held, by what date, and on what exact scope? Request the certificate, not the logo.
  4. Is a data processing agreement proposed and does it provide for a realistic audit right?

How to read the answers. a precise and limited answer is better than a reassuring and general answer. “Treatment in Switzerland only in this mode, abroad with these engines” is a good answer. “Your data is secure” is not one.

What you can decide this week

  • classify your documents into three baskets: This can be translated by any engine, which requires national treatment, which is not out of the office. Most organizations have never done this exercise, and it is taking a meeting.
  • give a default approved tool. until it exists, the default behavior is the nearest online service.
  • write the rule as a choice, no ban: in this case, this mode; in this other mode. A rule that begins with “it is forbidden to” does not say what to do.
  • Check the answers to the above questions once a year: engines, subcontractors and terms change faster than contracts.

Finally, a word about texts. Data protection is governed by the Federal Act on data Protection and by the European Regulation where data subjects in the Union are involved; professional secrecy of lawyers is governed by federal criminal law and professional regulations. These frameworks do not replace each other and their application to a given tool depends on the circumstances. This article describes a practice of prudence; it does not replace the opinion of your compliance manager or professional order.

Frequently asked Questions

What is the difference between storage and data processing?

Storage is the location where the file stays permanently. Treatment is the place where it is read and calculated, even for a few seconds. A tool can easily store your documents on Swiss servers while sending every sentence to an engine hosted abroad to translate it. Both answers must be obtained separately, and the second depends on the engine selected for each shipment.

Is pseudonymisation of a document sufficient to remove the risk?

No, it reduces it without eliminating it. Removing the names of the parties leaves the context unchanged: a singular transaction, an unusual clause, a combination of dates and jurisdictions may be sufficient to re-identify a case file. Pseudonymisation is a useful precautionary measure, not an anonymisation, and it never dispenses with knowing where the text is being processed.

Why is a warning displayed in the tool better than an internal Charter?

Because it appears at the time of the decision to the person making the decision. A Charter signed upon arrival is forgotten on the day forty pieces have to be translated before a hearing. In Corrext, the choice of an engine hosted abroad reveals the Attorney-Client privilege opinion, which recalls the obligation to pseudonymiser and the prohibition to use these engines for sensitive files.

What questions do translation providers have about confidentiality?

Where the storage servers are and who operates them, where the processing is carried out for each proposed engine, which contractors are involved, what data is reused and for what purpose, how long the contents are stored and how they are deleted, what certification is held and on what scope, and what rights apply to the contract.

Does ISO 27001 certification guarantee that my data remains in Switzerland?

No. An information security certification certifies that a management system exists and has been audited within a given perimeter. It alone says nothing about the location of treatments. Ask for the certificate, its date, its exact scope, and ask the question of the place of treatment for each proposed engine separately.
