Guide. prepare a multi-lingual annual report

An annual report in three or four languages is not a document that translates three times: it is a single document that must say the same thing everywhere, on the same date, with the same figures. This guide describes the only method that works: from publication to escalation.

Multilingual annual reports rarely fail on translation. They fail on the calendar, on a source text that continues to move, and on numbers that no longer match two versions. The following seven steps address these three causes in the order in which they arise.

1. Starting from publication and escalating

The publication date is the only non-negotiable data of the project: it is set by the general meeting, by a regulatory obligation or by a financial communication calendar. The rest can be inferred from this. So, build the schedule backwards, milestone by milestone, starting from the end.

  • publication simultaneous dissemination of all language versions. Simultaneously is not a comfort: an advance version creates an information asymmetry.
  • Certificate to be drawn written agreement of the validator of each language on an identified and dated version. No changes after this point.
  • Replay of prints Control over the page document, not on the translation file: csures, overflows, tables, references, table of contents.
  • layout Report validated text in the model, in each language. Make sure that languages are not the same length.
  • language Replay Reecture by a translator or legal-linguist, at the level chosen for each section.
  • Translation After freezing source text, never before.
  • freezing source text Project cutover point. See step 2.
  • fixed terminology Glossary agreed and disseminated to all participants.
  • drafting and closing numbers producing source text and stopping encrypted data.

Two construction rules are more valid than any estimate of duration.

  • a step cannot start before the end of the previous, except layout, which can start on previously validated sections.
  • the durations do not add up linearly when adding a language. the translation parallels, but the validation, layout and replay of prints are multiplying. Count the coordination effort, not just the translation effort.

To encrypt translation and replay steps, use the standard page volume and the chosen level of replay rather than a print: this is how a quotation is calculated, and our article on reading a quotation details what each level covers.

2. Freeze Source text

This is the most cost-effective and difficult decision of the project. Freezing the source text means that, at a given date, the reference document will no longer change, and any subsequent amendment will require an explicit procedure.

The reason is arithmetic. A correction of a sentence after the start of translations must be reflected in each language, reproduced in each language, deferred in a model and checked on a test basis. the cost of a late correction is not proportional to the number of words, but to the number of languages multiplied by the number of steps already taken.

For the gel to hold, there are three conditions.

  1. It is dated and announced in advance, at the launch of the project, to all persons likely to propose a change, including management.
  2. It is preceded by a source review window that is sufficiently broad for everyone to actually be able to intervene. A freeze without a prior window is a gel that will jump.
  3. It provides for its own exception. define what justifies a post-freeze change (factual error, regulatory change, post-closure event), which allows it, and through which channel it is propagated. Without exception, the first emergency destroys the rule.

the change log. keep a list of changes occurring after freezing: date, section, reason, translated languages, validator. It is used twice: to check that no language has been forgotten, and to show the next year what the late freeze cost.

3. Set terminology before first sentence translated

An annual report is based on a limited and repeated vocabulary: names of the governing bodies, titles of the functions, names of the entities, items in the financial statements, regulatory designations. If this vocabulary is not stopped before translation, it will be tacitly stopped by each translator, differently.

Build a project glossary with, for each entry: the source term, the term chosen in each target language, the source of the decision, and the date. Three families of newcomers deserve special attention.

  • Official names: company name, entities within the perimeter, statutory bodies. They are not freely translated; repeat the form entered in the Register and the practices already published.
  • the regulatory and legal terms. in Switzerland, they already exist in the three official languages, in the texts published: that is where they are taken, and not elsewhere. The abbreviations of laws change according to language, as detailed in our article on legal quadrilingualism.
  • recurring company formulations: Strategy title, program name, slogan. Decide whether they are translated or not, and then make the decision throughout the document.

In order to verify a term of Swiss law in its official context rather than in a dictionary, a bilingual concordance backed by public sources, such as fast lookup CHnell, cheaply replaces manual return-and-return between portals.

4. Translate: who does what, in what order

Not all sections of an annual report have the same requirement, and treating them evenly amounts to either overpayment or risk. Cut the document.

  • recurring and stable sections (governance, description of activities, disclosures): they should not be retranslated. Repeat the versions published in the previous year and have the amended passages checked.
  • Editorial sections (management message, review of the exercise): they have a tone and an intent. They require a thorough review, with attention to style and register.
  • Financial and regulatory sections: they require strict terminological and numerical accuracy and a verification by a person familiar with the applicable accounting framework.
  • sections for an authority or official publication: they may require a duplicate review and a certificate, which is planned for a long time in advance because the tour involves third parties.

On the treatment order, two recommendations. complete with the stable sections, which treat themselves without waiting for full freezing and save time for the next steps. And translate the tables and their notes together with the current text that comments them: separated, they invariably differ.

5. Maintaining consistency between languages

There are four aspects to the coherence of a multilingual report, and it is useful to check them separately.

Terminology consistency

The same source term given by the same target term, everywhere, including titles, captions, table of contents and appendices. This is the plan that is easiest to use and most often neglected in the annexes.

Numerical consistency

Each digit in the current text must correspond to the table it comments in each language. A correction to a table after translation almost always leaves an orphan sentence in a version. Check digit to digit, language by language, after layout.

Structural coherence

Same number of sections, same order, same numbering, same internal cross references. A section deleted in one version and retained in another is an error that is difficult to catch up with once the document is published.

Consistency of tone

A proactive management message in French and cautious in German tells two stories. Have the editorial sections of the various languages read by the same bilingual person, at the same time, before validation.

a control that takes ten minutes. Compare tables of contents for all versions side by side at the end. differences in structure, numbering, and titles appear immediately, while they are invisible when linking one version to the other.

6. Review and validation

Review and validation are two distinct operations, and confusing them is the main cause of endless returns.

Replay judges language: accuracy, terminology, consistency, style. It shall be made by a translator or a lawyer-linguist at the level chosen for the section.

Validation judges the merits: is this what the company means? It shall be carried out by a line manager, one per language, named by name at the start of the project.

Four rules make this phase workable.

  1. a validator named by language, not a Committee. Collective approval without responsibility produces contradictory remarks that nobody has a mandate to decide.
  2. an identified and dated version. remarks should relate to a document that everyone knows is the right one.
  3. a stated validation period, after which the absence of a response shall be deemed to have been approved. Without this clause, validation becomes the gateway to the project.
  4. only one comment pass per validator. Second comments are only admissible if they relate to a change made in the meantime.

On the choice of the level of review per section, the reasoning is the same as for any legal document: it depends on the addressee. levels of quality range from the reecture of significant errors to the double reecture certified by a specialized agency.

7. Layout, formats and publication

The final step is where mistakes are seen, because they are finally seen on the actual document.

What the layout reveals

  • different lengths. languages do not take the same place. Make sure you breathe in the model as soon as you design it instead of reducing one body of text to the last minute in a single version.
  • csures. German produces long words and sometimes faulty cuts; visually check them, an AutoCorrector does not see them.
  • Cross-references and pagination. Table of contents, index, internal cross-references, and cross-references are recalculated in each version.

The Swiss formats to check

  • Separator of thousands per apostrophe, currency in CHF.
  • No et seqq. in Swiss German: always et seqq.
  • Dates written unambiguously in English versions, months in words.
  • Abbreviations of laws specific to each official language.
  • Names of Swiss authorities and jurisdictions, never their foreign homonymous names.

Publish, then capitalize

Once the report has been published, two gestures prepare for the following year and take only half a day.

  1. Archiving final versions in all languages, aligned section by section. These will serve as the basis for recurring sections of the next fiscal year.
  2. Consolidate project glossary in the organization's permanent glossary, with sources. This is the only way to ensure that this year's arbitrations are not reconsidered next year.

Finally, make a short assessment with the speakers: when did the freeze really take place, how many changes were made afterwards, and which could have been avoided. This page is the best starting point for the next schedule.

On the tool side, a multilingual annual report asks for three things: reliable volume counting for planning, explicit revision level per section, and verifiable terminology in official sources. Frequently asked Questions

Why should the source text of an annual report be frozen?

Because each change made after the translations are launched must be translated into all languages, read in each language and rechecked in layout. The cost of late correction is not proportional to the number of words changed, but to the number of languages multiplied by the number of steps already taken. A formal freeze, dated and communicated, is the most cost-effective measure of the project.

How can the timetable for a multilingual annual report be built?

Backwards from the date of publication. First place the publication and then go back: final validation, layout and replay of prints, language reinterpretation, translation, freezing of source text, fixed terminology, writing. Each previous milestone is constrained by the next, and freezing the source text is the point that affects all others.

How can consistency be ensured between language versions of a report?

By deleting terminology before the first sentence translated, by designating one responsible person per language, and by treating recurring sections from year to year from previously published versions. Blocks that do not change must not be translated again: they must be taken over and then checked.

Who has to validate translated versions of an annual report?

A professional validator per language, capable of judging the substance, distinct from the language relector who judges the language. The validation must relate to an identified and dated version and be concluded by written agreement. Collective approval without a named responsible person produces contradictory remarks that no one has a mandate to decide.

Which Swiss formats should be checked before publication?

The separator of the thousands by apostrophe, the currency in CHF, the absence of Swiss German references, unambiguous written dates in English versions, abbreviations of laws specific to each official language, and the numbered concordance between the tables and the current text, in each version.
