This language pair in practice

Swiss acts are authoritative in German, French and Italian; their English versions are published for information only and have no legal force. A French to English translation therefore serves to understand, negotiate or argue a case, and always refers back to the French text when a citation must be binding.

No English version is authoritative

English translations of federal acts are provided for information only. A binding citation stays in French, German or Italian, followed by its translation where useful.The caveat appears on the English translations published by the Confederation.

International arbitration

A Swiss seat of arbitration falls under the chapter on international arbitration of the Federal Act on Private International Law. Jurisdiction, award and appeal each have a fixed term there.An appeal against an award is brought before the Federal Supreme Court.

Citing a decision

A decision published in the official collection is cited by its reference, ATF or BGE, with its volume, part and page. Converting it into a common law style citation makes the decision impossible to find for the opposing party and for the tribunal.

The Federal Supreme Court

The Confederation's English texts call the institution the Federal Supreme Court. Supreme Court alone can be confused with other courts.

Concepts without an equivalent

Poursuite (debt enforcement), séquestre (attachment), ordonnance pénale (summary penalty order): these concepts have no exact counterpart in common law. They are rendered with an explanation, then with one consistent term throughout the case file.

Finance and reporting

Prospectuses, annual reports and compliance documents circulate in English. Swiss financial terminology can be checked in CHnell, which covers 30 domains drawn from official sources.

Volume in an arbitration

Exhibits run to hundreds of pages and often arrive as PDFs. Text and document translation accepts up to 25 files of 50 MB per upload and converts a scan into an editable Word document before processing.

Confidentiality

French and English are among the four languages of Highly sensitive content mode: storage and processing in Switzerland only, with no reuse as translation memory.

Frequently asked questions about French to English translation

Can I cite a Swiss act in English in a submission?

You can cite it in English for the reader's benefit, but the English version cannot be relied on for what the law says. English is not an official language of the Confederation, and the English versions of Swiss acts are published for information only. The safe approach is to cite the French, German or Italian text, then give its English translation.

How do I cite a Federal Supreme Court decision in English?

The reference is not translated: an ATF reference, BGE in German, keeps its form, with its volume, part and page, and the court is called the Federal Supreme Court. Only the quoted passage is translated, with a note that it is a courtesy translation.

Is arbitration vocabulary consistent from one case to the next?

It must above all be consistent within a single case file. Jurisdiction, award, appeal and interim measures each receive one term, used in every exchange. Fast lookup CHnell shows the official usage of each term, and your own resources sit alongside the public sources.

What about concepts that do not exist in common law?

Keep them and explain them. Poursuite (debt enforcement), séquestre (attachment) and ordonnance pénale (summary penalty order) have no exact counterpart: the Swiss term is kept, explained at its first occurrence, then reused as it is. Translating them with a similar common law concept creates a mistranslation that the reader cannot see.

Where are the documents processed?

On Swiss servers in every case. With LexMachina or Highly sensitive content mode, processing stays in Switzerland only and the content is not reused as memory. Third-party engines hosted abroad trigger the Attorney-Client privilege notice and assume prior pseudonymisation.
